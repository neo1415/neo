 EXECUTIVE READING GUIDE


 Decision summary
 The central finding: AI sourcing and interview automation are no longer under-served categories. TalentQ
 must win on measurable hiring outcomes, verifiable mutual fit, trusted candidate experiences and
 distribution in a chosen customer segment, not on a claim that competitors lack agents. [S03,S04,S05]


     WHAT WE CAN SAY TODAY

     A real, growing recruitment technology market exists. Employers and candidates report persistent failures in
     matching, speed, trust, feedback and candidate conversion. Strong incumbents already sell AI workflow
     automation. TalentQ is an unvalidated entrant with a credible product thesis but no demonstrated proprietary
     data advantage.




 Six decisions this report supports
 • Category: evidence-led hiring orchestration, not another job board and not a general HR super-app.

 • Beachhead: test specialist agencies and mid-market employers hiring repeat professional roles; compare
 buying cycles and retention.

 • Product wedge: a qualified, interested, permissioned shortlist with full candidate-recruiter follow-through.

 • Monetization: employer-funded subscription / mandate pricing, with metered high-cost assessment and
 video services.

 • Expansion: preserve global architecture, deploy regionally in deliberate phases after compliance and data
 partnerships.

 • Investability: raise against proof milestones, not broad market size or invented model accuracy.


 Reliability statement
 Sources are dated and cited as [Sxx]. Third-party market-research values are publisher estimates, not
 independently auditable census measurements. Company feature claims are company statements; they are
 not independent benchmark results. All financial outputs are assumption-driven scenarios. Interviews,
 paid-user conversion, retention and actual candidate outcome measurement remain open fieldwork.


 What is outside the scope of this document
 A live financial audit of competitors, authenticated testing of paid ATS accounts, legal opinions for every
 jurisdiction, primary employer interviews, signed data licensing terms, and a validated TalentQ algorithm. The
 next investor deck must not imply these exist.

 Do not inflate market size
 Recruiting agency fees, staffing placement revenues, job advertising, applicant-tracking software, video
 interviews, AI sourcing and assessment subscriptions all sit at different layers of the value chain. A top-down
 market estimate is only useful when the product can plausibly capture that spend. Many industry estimates
 count overlapping buyers and spend categories. TalentQ cannot add them, and cannot assume all staffing
 revenue is software-addressable.


 Demand exists even when vacancies fall
 The US and UK indicators show substantial ongoing hiring, but the UK vacancy count is falling compared
 with earlier periods. This suggests that TalentQ should not assume a constantly accelerating hiring market.
 Lower hiring volumes can reduce recruiting budgets while increasing pressure on employers to avoid poor
 hires. It may reward efficiency software, or delay new procurement. Both outcomes need scenario testing.
 [S09,S10]

 Entry-market options, not a false global ranking
 • Nigerian specialist agencies: founder network access could aid pilots, but margins and spending power
 need testing against low-price incumbents.

 • UK recruitment boutiques: strong agency process fit, software budgets and possible bilingual reach;
 GDPR/ICO obligations and sales cycle require diligence.

 • US niche recruitment operators: highest potential contract value, crowded stack and tougher verification /
 security requirements.

 • Employer-led growth: mid-market HR teams may pay more but need ATS integrations and longer
 procurement.


 Global-by-design, focused-by-deployment
 Build regional configuration for currencies, consent policies, candidate outreach, local data retention,
 languages, tax and payment systems. Do not run unrestricted worldwide scraping or assume data source
 rights transfer internationally. Country expansion should be gated on legal review, employer density,
 profitable acquisition and enough opted-in candidate evidence.

 Interview guide: 15 diagnostic questions
 01. How many roles and placements did you handle in the last 90 days?
 02. What do you pay for listings, ATS, sourcing, assessments and recruiters?
 03. How many recruiter-hours from mandate to interview-worthy shortlist?
 04. What was your last failed hire or abandoned search? Why?
 05. How often are top-ranked prospects uncontactable or uninterested?
 06. What source can legally be exported or connected to a new tool?
 07. How are compensation, work arrangements and candidate interest checked?
 08. What is the cost of an unfilled role for you?
 09. Which two workflow steps would you pay to eliminate this quarter?
 10. What concerns would stop you authorizing Q to contact a candidate?
 11. Which human decisions must remain manual?
 12. What evidence would convince you Q improves decisions?
 13. Who approves purchase, and how long does approval take?
 14. Would you pay $129 per role, $349 per team/month, or $799 per agency/month?
 15. Will you pay for a limited pilot now, and provide anonymized baseline measurements?

 CHAPTER 06


 Differentiation & moat construction
 The standalone "why us"
 TalentQ is a recruiting outcome company, not an existing assistant with a new wrapper. Its proposed
 differentiation is the integrated method by which it converts a hiring need into an evidence-backed,
 candidate-compatible, auditable recruitment process. Q is its conversational and agent interface; the
 defensible asset, if earned, is the network, consented evidence, feedback quality, assessment calibration and
 employer execution reliability.


 What is not a moat
 • Voice chat, video avatars, generic LLM candidate summaries, basic vector search, an ATS pipeline, a
 mathematical score, scraped LinkedIn profiles, or a large set of flashy agents. Competitors already offer many
 of these and can replicate the rest.

 CHAPTER 07


 Market sizing without false precision
 Top-down reference numbers
 Publisher estimates: online recruitment technology $17.48bn (2026); AI recruitment $5.1bn (2026). They
 are not additive and do not represent automatically accessible TalentQ revenue. Both are sold through many
 products, user bases and price models. These may change with publisher methodology. [S01,S02]


 Bottom-up serviceable market equation
 Annual potential = eligible organizations × reachable share × likely annual spend. For an initial
 non-enterprise subscription market, organization counts must eventually come from company registries,
 recruitment memberships and qualified buyer lists, not from global SME totals. All parameter values below
 are deliberately hypothetical.

   Scenario            Reachable qualifying orgs        Annual spend / org         Gross annual opportunity
                       (illustrative)


   Constrained         20,000                           $1,000                     $20m


   Planning            60,000                           $3,000                     $180m


   Expansive           150,000                          $6,000                     $900m


 Do not call $180m a verified SAM. It is an assumption-driven planning scenario. Region and segment
 organization counts, reachable proportions, purchase propensity and annual contract values must be
 validated. Also, these opportunity estimates are not additive to the category-level report figures.


 Near-term obtainable market (SOM)
 A starting commercial target might be 200 paying organizations at $2,400 annual recurring spend each,
 yielding $480,000 annualized recurring revenue if all are active at period end. This is an operating milestone,
 not a forecast of adoption. If users purchase only isolated mandates, recognize actual contract value, not
 annualized subscription revenue.


 Bottom-up funnel: a falsifiable model
 Qualified accounts = reachable organizations × contactability × response × meeting rate × paid conversion.
 Example: 8,000 reachable × 35% contactable × 12% reply × 35% meeting × 25% close ≈ 29 accounts. Every
 percentage is a scenario assumption and must be replaced with CRM data after early sales.


 Implication for venture-scale ambition
 A $17bn+ category supports a large incumbent ecosystem. It does not prove TalentQ can become a
 billion-dollar company. Venture-scale outcomes require sustained acquisition, repeat usage, expanding ACV,
 gross margin discipline and defensibility beyond a generic agent interface. A business reaching a few million
 dollars of ARR can still be worthwhile without being venture-scale.

 Business model trade-offs
 • Per-mandate: easy to try and align to hiring jobs; volatile recurring revenue and support load.

 • Seat subscription: useful for agencies, but exposed to recruiter headcount reductions and seat-sharing.

 • Per successful hire: aligns outcomes, but creates disputes over attribution, exclusivity, replacement and
 legal placement-fee regimes.

 • Hybrid usage: best protects AI / sourcing gross margins, but adds pricing complexity.

 • Candidate premium: optional job-preparation services only; never charge simply to be considered or to
 correct personal data.


 The MVP monetization experiment
 Run a randomized offer menu among comparable pilot leads: mandate fee versus team subscription versus
 subscription plus usage. Measure signed payment and repeat renewal rather than stated willingness to pay.
 Publish pricing only after cost caps and acceptable cancellation terms are established.

 Published AI service anchors
 As of the source checks, OpenAI publishes GPT-Live at $0.05 per minute for voice-front-end sessions, with
 backend model/tool usage billed separately. Tavus lists conversational video overages around $0.35/min at
 the Builder tier, with a 30-second minimum. Plans and terms may change. [S19,S20]

 minutes at $0.35 is $7.00 before any other charges. These are unit-cost illustrations, not all-in prices. Always

 CAC, churn and payback model
 Example: $1,100 acquisition cost ÷ $256 monthly gross contribution = 4.3-month gross-contribution
 payback before fixed corporate expenses. At an assumed 5% monthly logo churn and unchanged margin,
 simple undiscounted gross-contribution LTV ≈ $256 / 0.05 = $5,120, or 4.7× CAC. But 5% monthly churn
 implies approximately 46% annual customer loss if not offset by acquisitions. It is not a healthy retention
 claim.

 CHAPTER 13


 Investor diligence & capital return logic
 How an investor gets their money back
 Venture equity normally returns capital through a secondary sale, acquisition, IPO or cash distributions if
 legally and financially appropriate. There is no scheduled repayment unless a financing agreement expressly
 creates one. Revenue growth is not cash available to investors.


 Illustrative year-five value sensitivity
 If base-case year-five subscription revenue is $14.26m, a hypothetical revenue-based valuation sensitivity of
 3×–6× gives an enterprise-value illustration of $42.8m–$85.5m. These multiples are purely scenario inputs,
 not market-validated 2026 transaction comparables. Year-five revenue is used as a simplifying proxy; actual
 exit pricing depends on growth, quality, recurring mix, profitability, retention, debt, cash and market
 conditions.

 If a hypothetical shareholder owns 8% fully diluted at exit, the simple gross equity-value illustration is
 ~$3.42m–$6.84m before preferred security terms, further dilution, liquidation preference, transaction costs
 and tax. It is not an IRR, guaranteed outcome or investable promise.


 What would justify the story
 • A signed, audited product and data-rights foundation.

 • Real paid pilot cohorts with baseline-controlled recruiter-time and shortlist comparisons.

 • Clear month-by-month conversion, retention, unit costs and burn.

 • Role-by-role assessment validity evidence and human decision governance.

 • A replicable distribution channel with measured CAC and cash payback.

 • A standalone reason employers choose TalentQ over their existing stack.
