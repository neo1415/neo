 TECHNICAL SERIES / DOCUMENT 01



 Document purpose
 A proposed technical specification for a standalone AI-native recruitment platform. It is intended
 to guide architecture, experimentation, product requirements and future investor diligence. It is
 not evidence of a deployed, validated or patented matching system.

   Independent product narrative
   TalentQ stands on its own merits. The external product identity, mission, performance
   claims and investment narrative should not depend on another application. Internally,
   engineering teams may reuse existing governed Q capabilities where security,
   performance and modularity tests justify doing so.



 Decisions this document makes
  • Defines a candidate-evidence system with provenance, freshness and candidate permissions.
  • Separates job eligibility, employer competency fit and candidate preference fit.
  • Specifies a transparent Q-Match baseline with missing-data treatment, uncertainty and
    ranking diagnostics.
  • Defines agent scopes, interview orchestration, measurable speed budgets and reliability
    controls.
  • Sets validation gates before claims about predictive accuracy, fairness, autonomous operation
    or defensibility.

 Decisions it deliberately does not make
  • Market size, realistic pricing, competitor economics, country-by-country willingness to pay or
    an investor return multiple. These require the dedicated market report.
  • Legal approval for any jurisdiction; the technical controls here require qualified legal review.
  • A claim that a mathematical equation or a model is patentable, unique or already trained.
  • Any promise of unrestricted LinkedIn data access or instant completion of every external
    search.


 Notation: F = employer-side competency fit; A = candidate-side compatibility; M = mutual-fit index; C =
 evidence coverage; U = uncertainty; S = optimized shortlist. Unless explicitly labeled as sourced facts, all
 thresholds, examples and architecture choices are proposed design parameters.

 TalentQ has four users: employer, agency recruiter, candidate and human reviewer. Each
 interacts with one governed conversational system backed by deterministic recruiting services
 and traceable evidence.

   North-star technical principle
   An LLM understands requirements and explains evidence. Deterministic services validate
   permissions, calculate scores, record events and enforce transitions. AI agents may
   propose or execute authorized tasks, but cannot silently become the final hiring authority.



 Seven architectural invariants
 • No ranking without a defined role, versioned rubric and source-traceable evidence.
 • No cross-tenant sharing without explicit access rights; candidate-owned information stays
   permission-scoped.
 • No surprise candidate outreach, background investigation or reuse of private practice
   recordings.
 • Unknown, stale and contradicted evidence remain visibly uncertain.
 • Human review remains meaningful for consequential selection decisions.
 • All important outputs are reproducible from their data, model, policy and rubric versions.
 • Every screen responds promptly even when external discovery or verification is still working.

 Evidence reliability

   r(e) = v(e) × f(e) × d(e)


   v = validation quality, f = freshness discount, d = evidence relevance; all in [0,1]. Proposed heuristic,
   not a population-calibrated probability.

 Freshness may use an exponential decay where appropriate: f(e) = exp[-ln(2) × age / half-life].
 The half-life depends on evidence type. A work sample may be relevant for longer than a phone
 number or job-availability declaration.

 Conflict handling
  • If two sources disagree about employment dates, show the conflicting claims and request
    clarification.
  • Never silently rewrite third-party facts from an LLM inference.
  • For de-duplication, require positive identity evidence; reversible merge operations are
    mandatory.
  • Candidate edits do not falsify external records; they create correction events and review flags.

 06 / RECOMMENDATION ARCHITECTURE



 Q-Match is a pipeline, not a magic score
         1                 2                  3                  4                  5                  6

       Gate            Retrieve           Evidence            Match             Diversify           Explain


      Rights            Exact +           Coverage           Employer             Top-k            Citations
    & eligibility      semantic           & quality         + candidate           slate             & gaps




 Stage 1: safe retrieval
 Apply data entitlements and legitimate hard filters first. Retrieve candidate IDs via structured
 predicates (e.g., geography, certification where mandatory) plus lexical and vector search. Hybrid
 retrieval can use reciprocal-rank fusion:


   RRF(i) = Σ_s [1 / (k + rank_s(i))]


   s indexes retrieval systems; k is a positive smoothing constant chosen on retrieval benchmarks (e.g.,
   tested values around 60).


 RRF is only a candidate-generation mechanism. It does not assert a candidate is qualified. Scores
 from different sources must not be treated as comparable without normalization and testing.

 Stage 2: evidence-based reranking
 • Build feature vectors from permitted competency evidence and preferences.
 • Use deterministic scoring formulas initially; compare to validated alternative models later.
 • Record exclusion reason, unresolved requirements, evidence coverage and candidate-interest
   state.
 • Generate explanations only from cited facts and computed features; never free-form infer
   hidden traits.

   Product contract
   The UI should show best documented match among the searched and permitted pool, not
   best person in Lagos. Source coverage is necessarily incomplete.



 07 / CORE MATHEMATICS



 Employer-side competency fit
 Every role has n job-relevant competency dimensions. The weights w_j are non-negative and sum
 to 1. Candidate demonstrations are evaluated against a role-specific rubric, not compared against
 personal characteristics.


   Σ(j=1..n) w_j = 1; 0 ≤ s_ij, c_ij, μ_j ≤ 1


   s_ij = demonstrated competency level; c_ij = evidence confidence; μ_j = prespecified neutral
   missing-evidence baseline.



   F_i = 100 × Σ_j w_j [c_ij s_ij + (1-c_ij) μ_j]


   F_i = provisional employer-fit index, 0–100. This is an interpretable shrinkage heuristic, not a
   probability of successful performance.



   C_i = Σ_j w_j c_ij; U_i = 1 - C_i


   C_i = weighted evidence coverage; U_i = unresolved evidence fraction.

 Why two separate numbers matter
 A candidate with an attractive F score but low C has an uncertain evaluation. The UI must
 present both. We do not multiply confidence by fit and then portray missing data as poor
 performance. The baseline μ is a provisional method that must be tuned and audited for
 differential effects.

 Role weight construction
 Start with job analysis: pairwise importance comparisons or a structured weighting workshop
 with an employer and a subject-matter expert. Cap any one dimension from dominating without
 documented justification. Version the rubric and compare inter-rater agreement before release.

 Example competency rubric

   Investigate alerts                       0.40             Scenario with investigation notes

   Query security logs                      0.30             Practical log task

   Communicate incident findings            0.20             Structured report rubric

   Document containment decisions           0.10             Incident discussion

 This example is a design illustration only. Weights and competency definitions are not assumed valid across
 all SOC employers.

 08 / TWO-SIDED MATCHING



 Candidate compatibility and mutual fit
 The employer fit F asks whether evidence supports the role requirements. Candidate
 compatibility A asks whether the opportunity fits the candidate’s own stated requirements.
 Neither can replace explicit interest.


   A_i = 100 × [Σ_k v_k a_ik / Σ_k v_k]


   a_ik [0,1] for known candidate preference k. Unknowns must be shown as unknown and omitted
   from the numerator and denominator only with an explicit low-coverage warning.


 Compatibility dimensions can include candidate-stated salary expectations, location, permitted
 work format, desired schedule and career direction. Separate exact constraints from negotiable
 preferences.


   M_i = √(F_i × A_i)


   The geometric mean penalizes one-sided matches; it is a heuristic index, not the probability a
   candidate will join or succeed.




 Crucial distinction: salary fit versus protected attributes
 Compensation compatibility is job-related and candidate-controlled. Age, sex, ethnicity, religion,
 pregnancy, disability or proxies for protected characteristics are not inputs to routine Q-Match
 ranking. If a lawful occupational exception exists, it requires jurisdiction-specific review outside
 the ordinary scoring pipeline.

 What happens when salary is unknown?
 Do not fill it from social media or past salary. Mark A as provisional and ask the candidate

 10 / SLATE DESIGN



 Shortlist optimization: not just top 15 scores
 Naively selecting the 15 highest-index candidates can create a redundant shortlist of people with
 nearly identical backgrounds. A better objective covers relevant capabilities and distinct
 approaches without manipulating protected demographics.


   S* = argmax_|S|≤K [Σ_(i∈S) M_i + λ D(S) - η R(S)]


   D = job-relevant coverage of distinct competencies/experience; R = redundancy among
   near-duplicate profiles; λ and η chosen by experiments.


 Use a transparent greedy maximal marginal relevance approximation for an initial
 implementation. Re-rank with evidence and policy checks after every new mandate version or
 material evidence update.

 Minimum operational controls
  • Do not let diversity optimization import protected attributes into individual scores.
  • Allow a reviewer to inspect exactly which job-relevant dimension each candidate adds.
  • Use consistent score versions within any shortlist comparison.
  • Respect candidate visibility and representation rights across agencies.
  • Never inflate an empty candidate pool into the requested K results.

 The stopping condition
 The system can return fewer than K candidates. Its explanation should identify whether the
 limiting factor was legal constraints, missing sources, poor evidence, compensation mismatch or
 confirmed unwillingness to engage. These are very different outcomes.

 11 / MODEL OUTPUT CONTRACT



 Uncertainty, explanation and human review
 Every recommendation should be explainable as a chain of evidence. The language model renders
 the explanation; deterministic code controls the facts and the calculated values.

  Output field             Example                                Control

  Summary                  "Strong log-investigation evidence"    Bound to an observed work
                                                                  sample

  Supporting evidence      Assessment A-104, timestamp, rubric    Clickable provenance
                           v3

  Unverified item          "Employer dates not verified"          Explicit uncertainty

  Missing requirement      "No evidence of cloud detection"       No negative personal inference

  Compatibility            "Hybrid arrangement confirmed"         Candidate-confirmed timestamp

  Next action              "Invite for specialist review"         No automatic rejection



 Human-in-the-loop is not a checkbox
 Reviewers need adequate evidence, time, authority and the ability to override recommendations.
 A nominal approval click on a concealed AI decision is not meaningful oversight. UK privacy
 guidance and Dutch regulator guidance discuss the importance of meaningful human
 involvement [S5, S6].

 Counterfactual tests
 For a candidate matched to a job, ask which job-relevant evidence would legitimately change the
 assessment. Example: obtaining an evaluated incident-response work sample could address a skill
 evidence gap. Never advise a candidate to change protected or irrelevant personal
 characteristics.

   Trust rule
   Separate three things: facts (source-backed), judgments (rubric-backed and contestable)
   and forecasts (calibrated only after sufficient data). The UI must not blur them.

 12 / STATISTICAL LEARNING PLAN



 From heuristics to validated prediction
 Hand-designed Q-Match indices establish a testable baseline, not predictive authority. A later
 model can learn probabilities of specific outcomes when enough representative, lawfully
 processed data exists.


   p_t(i,j) = P(Y_t = 1 | X_ij, segment, time)


   Possible t: qualified at review, interview accepted, offer accepted. Keep separate outcomes rather
   than collapsing them into a vague "hireability" number.




 Data requirements
  • A pre-registered outcome definition and clear time window for each model.
  • Training labels from observed events, with reasons for missing outcomes and right-censoring.
  • Recruitment-market and job-family stratification; no assumed transportability between
    Nigeria, UK and US.
  • Leakage prevention: no post-interview events as features when predicting pre-interview
    suitability.
  • Temporal holdout splits, reviewer-blind datasets and prospective validation before use in
    consequential decisions.

 14 / EVALUATION ORCHESTRATION



 Q interviews and specialist assessments
 Tavus-class video avatars, low-latency speech and natural-language interviewing are interfaces,
 not substitutes for assessment science. Candidate outcomes should rest on observable
 job-related answers and work products.

 Four evaluation modes

  Mode                           Appropriate role                  Infrastructure

  Structured text or voice       Sales, service, administration    Question bank + rubric + transcript

  Live conversational scenario   Business development, support     Realistic customer/problem roleplay

  Sandbox work sample            Engineering, operations,          Constrained IDE/log/SQL/document
                                 cybersecurity                     tasks

  Specialist provider            Advanced cyber, regulated roles   Secure third-party integration and
  assessment                                                       review



 Interview protocol
 • Notify the candidate of AI participation, processing purposes, recordings and available
   alternative arrangements.
 • Anchor questions to the approved role rubric. Ask follow-ups that clarify evidence, not
   protected traits.
 • Timestamp transcript turns and assessment artifacts; separate raw observation from evaluator
   judgment.
 • Use human evaluation where required; provide recourse for technical issues and
   accommodation needs.
 • Maintain candidate coaching/rehearsal data separately from employer hiring evidence.

   Cybersecurity example
   A Level-1 SOC applicant receives synthetic sign-in logs and a mock alert. The candidate
   explains triage and writes an incident note. Q records time-stamped steps and maps work
   to an approved rubric. A qualified analyst reviews the evidence. TalentQ does not claim to
   replicate a complete SIEM or outperform specialist cyber ranges.

 Performance design
  • Precompute embeddings and candidate competency vectors; update incrementally with

 21 / TECHNICAL ECONOMICS



 Costs, unit budgets and scale
 The market research report will estimate real provider costs and competitive pricing. This
 engineering model defines what to instrument before proposing margins.


   Cost(m) = Σ_p units(m,p) × rate(p) + storage(m) + egress(m) + review(m)


   p includes model tokens, voice minutes, video sessions, enrichment calls, assessment provider
   events and message delivery. Rates are contractual and geography-dependent.



   Gross contribution(m) = Revenue(m) - direct variable cost(m)


   Exclude sunk product R&D; include paid human review when it is needed to fulfil the mandate.




 Cost-control mechanisms
  • Use smaller models for extraction and candidate-format normalization; reserve stronger
    reasoning for exceptional ambiguity and explanation review.
  • Deduplicate enrichment events and avoid full reindexing on minor changes.
  • Meter premium video interviews, verification and specialist assessment usage.
  • Place per-tenant and per-mandate quotas on sourcing queries, outreach and models.
  • Prefer cached evidence snapshots for repeat recruiter interactions, with visible freshness
    labels.
  • Require manual authorization before unusually expensive agent plans.

 Scale architecture
 A modular-monolith baseline reduces operational overhead. Split the ingestion worker, search
 cluster or interview media subsystem only when performance and risk telemetry demonstrates
 the need. Regional tenancy, data residency and processor-contract support should exist in the
 schema before international deployment.

                                           constraints.
