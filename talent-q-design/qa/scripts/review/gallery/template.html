Across every page

What changed in the shared layer

These fixes live in the components every journey uses, so each one improved dozens of screens at once.

Pages arrive, they don’t pop

One motion vocabulary (durations and easing as tokens) for page arrival, Q’s replies and cards. Lite mode and reduced-motion turn it off, and no animation ever blocks a click.

Q’s cards read at a glance

Suggestions, approvals and drafts share one structure: what Q noticed or made, the action, then a quiet footer with “Why am I seeing this?”, Later and Dismiss. One card shows at a time; the rest wait one tap away.

Status is a tag, not a title

Headers carry one line of context and a title. States like Draft, Verified or Waiting moved into tags and stage strips, so titles say what the page is.

Lists became sheets of rows

Floating card walls were replaced by one surface with whole-row links and a “next” line, which scans faster and fits a phone.

Settings are real switches

On/off choices use one accessible switch row with the state in words. Choices with several options stay as segmented buttons.

Phones first, not phones later

Tabs wrap instead of hiding, banners stack their action under the text, the Ask Q bar and tab bar never cover content, and Q’s replies are set larger on small screens.

Privacy holds everywhere

Pages are guarded by organisation, not hidden from the menu. Hidden people are counted, never named. Q refuses without revealing whether anything exists.

Nothing depends on a browser dialog

Reset, cancelling a plan and cancelling an agent now confirm in place, so they work inside this page too.

Still weak · most important first

What isn’t good enough yet

Each area’s designer listed what they couldn’t fix. Some were shared problems and are now fixed; the rest are below.

Design

  1. Some phone pages are still longThe rubric studio and Tobi’s profile now have a section index, but they remain long. The video transcript scrolls inside the page on phones.
  2. Page header crowdingWith long titles, the header’s actions drop to a second row; on a phone, a job’s Save button sits above the content instead of beside the title.
  3. Q’s longer replies on phonesFraunces at 18 px reads well in short lines but long answers get heavy. Long replies should summarise first and fold the rest.
  4. Cards can still echo the pageQ’s opening line no longer repeats the page, but a proactive card can still restate something the page shows (Grace’s interview).
  5. Agency at scaleAgent rows are long on phones, there’s no summary mode for ten or more agents, and the client switcher won’t work past about eight clients.
  6. Pipeline filters on phonesRole filters wrap to three rows at 390 px. A select would be tighter.
  7. Career atlas densityAt 48 occupations the radial labels crowd. The list view is the fallback, but the map should cluster.
  8. Roles list “next” lineIt’s text only. A small stage track per role would scan faster.

Behaviour and product

  1. The score isn’t validatedRubric weights are now set per role, but the index is the addendum’s prototype arithmetic. It needs job analysis and adverse-impact checks before it informs decisions (D17).
  2. No real analysis yetVideo findings, transcripts and reply summaries are written in advance or rule-based. Any upload yields the same demo transcript; the page says so.
  3. Gaps the builders listedNo “start the next round” after keeping v1; owners and due dates aren’t editable; a person in two roles shows once in the hub; public video preview isn’t built.
  4. Interview copilot timingIt doesn’t track time per question, and its question rail duplicates Previous/Next on desktop.
  5. Practice tips repeatTips are rule-based and feel the same across questions. A real model would vary them.
  6. Handbook rules are guidanceRules from an uploaded handbook are cited, not machine-checked. Classifying them is production work.
  7. Agency registration has no screenHow a candidate registers with an agency and what they consent to is still seed data (decision D2).
  8. The intake brief has no versions viewKora’s intake page edits the older mandate record, so unlike Funmi’s briefs it has no version history.

Fixed during review

  1. Generic Q greetingQ’s first line now names one real thing on the page and never repeats a card or the greeting.
  2. Fixed 1.5× weightingThe comparison uses the approved rubric’s weights, explained in the margin.
  3. Duplicate Q cardsTwo areas noticed the same interview and both showed a card. Now one card per subject, and dismissing it covers both.
  4. Card stacks pushing the conversation awayQ now shows its most urgent card and folds the rest.
  5. Reset and cancel did nothing when embeddedThey used browser dialogs, which this viewer blocks. They now confirm in place.
  6. Offline banner squeezed on phonesIts action now sits under the text.
  7. Settings sent you to the wrong place“Change who can find you” now opens the Passport’s search tab directly.
Your decisions · now in the product

What you decided, and where to see it

D1 · Discovery

Findable by anyone, with limits for the unverified

The default is that any organisation can find a candidate. Until verified, an organisation can send 3 messages a week, labelled as unverified, and can’t run campaigns, make submittals or see contact details. Open as Funmi to see it.

D3 · Verification

No posting until the business is verified

Funmi can draft and approve a brief and search straight away. Posting asks for her business registration number first; a wrong number fails honestly and every attempt is in the audit log.

D6 · Skills and jobs

ESCO, on the ILO’s ISCO-08 codes

The open European classification of about 3,000 occupations and 13,900 skills, linked to each other. The career atlas, matching and job tags use it in production; local job titles map onto it.

D16–D19 · New, for you

Four decisions the addenda raise

Mail access level (send-only first, or reading replies too); who validates rubrics and the score; which provider analyses video and how long it’s kept; and the default for candidates contacting employers. Details in the decision register.

D8 · Language

English now, built to translate

Every string goes through a message catalogue and dates and money use locale formatting, so adding a language is translation, not rework.
